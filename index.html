                Guide

                Using LeafScan well

                How to take a photo it can read, what its answers mean, and where to get a second opinion.

                Taking a good photo

                1. One leaf that fills most of the square, showing the side with the spots.
                2. Hold the phone flat above the leaf and keep still. Tilted or blurry photos are often wrong.
                3. Even light: shade or near a window, no harsh shadows or dark corners.
                4. Plain background if you can, such as paper, a table or your palm.
                5. If the result is not sure, add 2 or 3 leaves from the same plant. More leaves give a more reliable answer.

                Reading the result

                LeafScan was tested on 3,100 farm photos it had never seen. This is how often it was right at each level:

                High confidence 70% and above
                Right about 9 in 10 times. Still compare the signs before you act.
                Medium confidence 45 to 70%
                Right about 7 in 10 times. Answer the questions or add another leaf.
                Not sure below 45%
                Right only about 4 in 10 times. It does not mean the plant is healthy.
                Not recognized below 20%
                The photo may not be a leaf, or a plant LeafScan knows.

                Tell LeafScan the plant if it guessed wrong. Use “Wrong plant?” on the result. On the same farm photos, choosing the right plant raised accuracy from 72% to 88%.

                Getting a second opinion

                • UPLB Pest Clinic: send fresh sick leaves by courier, and they email a diagnosis report, usually within 7 working days. How to send a sample
                • DA Regional Crop Protection Center at your Department of Agriculture regional office. Some run a Plant Health Clinic.
                • An agriculture teacher, or the agriculture college of a state university near you.

                Use “Show to a technician” on a result to send the leaf photos and LeafScan's answer together. Use only pesticides registered with the Fertilizer and Pesticide Authority (FPA), following the label.

                About LeafScan

                A student research prototype: Developing a Prototype for Plant Disease Detection Using Image Recognition. A MobileNetV3 image recognition model runs entirely on your device with ONNX Runtime Web, so photos are never uploaded. Disease information is summarized from CABI, PlantVillage (Penn State) and university extension sources, with scientific names checked against GBIF. Summaries have not yet been reviewed by a plant pathologist. Non-commercial use only.
